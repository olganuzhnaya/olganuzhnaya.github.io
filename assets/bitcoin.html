Bitcoin

Bitcoin is a decentralized digital monetary network created in 2009. It uses cryptographic verification, a distributed ledger and Proof-of-Work consensus to enable peer-to-peer transfer of value without a central issuing authority.

Bitcoin Technology

Bitcoin is built around a distributed ledger commonly known as a blockchain. Transactions are grouped into blocks and linked cryptographically.

The Bitcoin network uses Proof-of-Work as its consensus mechanism. Miners compete to find a valid block according to the network's difficulty rules.

The security model combines cryptographic signatures, hashing, economic incentives and distributed verification.

Bitcoin transactions are digitally signed. A private key is used to authorize spending, while the corresponding public information allows the network to verify the signature.

Why Bitcoin Has a Maximum Supply of 21 Million

Bitcoin's protocol defines an issuance schedule that approaches a maximum supply of approximately 21 million BTC.

New bitcoins are introduced through the block subsidy paid to miners. The subsidy decreases periodically through events commonly called halvings.

This means that the rate at which new bitcoins enter circulation is determined by the protocol rather than by a central monetary authority.

The exact amount of bitcoin in existence at any particular moment depends on the blocks that have been mined and the protocol's issuance rules.

History of Bitcoin

In October 2008, a person or group using the name Satoshi Nakamoto published the paper Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System .

The Bitcoin network began operating in January 2009, when the first block, known as the Genesis Block, was created.

Bitcoin introduced a system in which participants could verify transactions through a distributed network rather than relying on a central clearing institution.
