What Are Layer-2 Networks?

Layer-2 networks, often called L2s, are blockchain systems designed to process transactions and computation while using an existing blockchain, such as Ethereum, as an important part of their underlying security and settlement infrastructure.

Layer-2 technology has become an important part of the effort to scale Ethereum. Instead of requiring every transaction to be processed directly on Ethereum's main network, some activity can be handled by a Layer-2 system and later represented or settled on Layer-1.

What Is Layer-1?

Layer-1 refers to the underlying blockchain itself. In the case of Ethereum, the Ethereum mainnet is Layer-1.

Layer-1 provides the core blockchain infrastructure, including consensus, transaction settlement, accounts, and the execution environment provided by the Ethereum Virtual Machine.

The term Layer-2 describes systems built on top of or in connection with Layer-1 that are designed to process activity more efficiently.

Why Does Ethereum Need Layer-2 Networks?

A public blockchain has finite computational and data resources. As more people use a network, competition for those resources can increase.

On Ethereum, increased demand can result in higher transaction fees and greater competition for block space.

Layer-2 networks are one approach to this scalability problem. They can process many transactions away from the Ethereum mainnet execution environment and then use Ethereum for settlement and security-related functions.

How Does Layer-2 Scaling Work?

The exact design depends on the Layer-2 system, but the basic idea is to move some transaction processing away from Layer-1.

Instead of sending every individual transaction directly through Ethereum, users can interact with a Layer-2 network.

The Layer-2 processes transactions and organizes the resulting information. The system then communicates relevant information or proofs back to Ethereum.

In simplified terms:

  1. A user sends a transaction to a Layer-2 network.
  2. The Layer-2 processes the transaction.
  3. Multiple transactions can be grouped together.
  4. Relevant transaction data or a proof is submitted to Ethereum.
  5. Ethereum provides the Layer-1 settlement and verification framework.

Real systems can be considerably more sophisticated than this simplified description.

Rollups

Rollups are a major category of Layer-2 scaling technology.

A rollup executes transactions outside the main Ethereum execution environment and then posts information to Ethereum that allows the resulting state to be verified or reconstructed according to the rollup's design.

Rollups are generally divided into two major categories: optimistic rollups and zero-knowledge rollups.

Optimistic Rollups

Optimistic rollups generally assume that submitted transaction results are valid unless someone challenges them within the applicable dispute process.

A fraud-proof or fault-proof mechanism can be used to challenge an invalid result.

This approach allows large numbers of transactions to be processed while relying on a mechanism through which incorrect results can be disputed.

Zero-Knowledge Rollups

Zero-knowledge rollups use cryptographic proofs to demonstrate that transactions have been processed according to the rules of the system.

Rather than relying primarily on a later challenge period, a zero-knowledge system can submit a validity proof that Ethereum or another verification system can check.

The underlying cryptography can be technically complex, but the central idea is relatively simple: a proof can demonstrate that a computation was performed correctly without requiring every observer to reproduce the entire computation in the same way.

What Does "Rollup" Mean?

The word "rollup" refers to the idea of processing many transactions together and representing their results in a more compact form for settlement on the underlying blockchain.

The details vary between implementations. Different rollups can make different choices about data availability, execution, sequencing, proof systems, and other technical components.

Layer-2 and Transaction Fees

One of the main reasons users interact with Layer-2 networks is transaction cost.

By processing many transactions together, a Layer-2 can distribute certain costs across a larger number of transactions.

This can make transactions less expensive than performing the same activity directly on a congested Layer-1 network.

However, Layer-2 transactions are not automatically free or guaranteed to remain cheaper under every condition. Fees depend on the particular network, its architecture, demand, and the costs associated with interacting with Layer-1.

Layer-2 and Ethereum Security

A key question when evaluating a Layer-2 network is how it uses Ethereum and what assumptions its security model requires.

Some Layer-2 systems are designed to inherit important security properties from Ethereum by posting transaction data and proofs to Ethereum.

But "built on Ethereum" does not mean that every Layer-2 has exactly the same security model as Ethereum itself.

Users should consider the specific architecture of a network, including its proof system, bridges, upgrade mechanisms, sequencing arrangements, data availability model, and other technical assumptions.

The Sequencer

Many Layer-2 systems use a sequencer to organize and order transactions before they are settled on Layer-1.

The sequencer can play an important operational role in the network's performance and user experience.

The degree of decentralization and the design of the sequencer can vary between Layer-2 systems.

Layer-2 Is Not the Same as a Sidechain

Layer-2 networks and sidechains are sometimes discussed together, but they are not necessarily the same thing.

A sidechain is generally an independent blockchain with its own consensus and security model.

A Layer-2 is designed to use an underlying Layer-1 in a more integrated way, particularly for settlement and security according to its architecture.

The distinction matters because moving assets or transactions between systems with different security assumptions can introduce additional risks.

Bridges and Layer-2 Networks

Bridges are mechanisms that allow assets or information to move between blockchain environments.

When users move assets between Ethereum and a Layer-2, a bridge mechanism is typically involved.

Bridge architecture can vary considerably. Smart contracts, validators, proof systems, and other mechanisms can all play roles depending on the design.

For this reason, users should understand that interacting with a bridge introduces its own technical considerations and potential risks.

Why Are There Many Layer-2 Networks?

Scaling Ethereum is not a single engineering problem with only one possible solution.

Different Layer-2 projects can make different design choices about execution, data availability, proof systems, decentralization, interoperability, fees, and user experience.

This has resulted in a broader ecosystem of Layer-2 networks, each with its own technical architecture and development model.

What Does Layer-2 Mean for Users?

For an ordinary user, the most visible difference can be the network selected when sending a transaction or using a decentralized application.

A wallet may allow a user to choose between Ethereum and one or more Layer-2 networks.

Before sending assets, users should check the selected network, the destination address, supported assets, transaction fees, and whether the receiving service supports that particular network.

Sending an asset using an unsupported network can create serious operational problems and, depending on the circumstances, may result in loss of access to the asset.

The Bigger Picture

Layer-2 networks illustrate an important principle in blockchain engineering: scalability often involves dividing responsibilities between different layers of a system.

Ethereum can serve as a settlement and security foundation while additional systems process larger amounts of activity.

This creates a broader architecture in which users, applications, Layer-2 networks, bridges, smart contracts, and Layer-1 infrastructure interact with one another.

Understanding this architecture makes it easier to understand why the modern crypto ecosystem is no longer organized around a single blockchain performing every task directly.

Key Takeaways

  • Layer-2 networks are designed to scale blockchain activity.
  • They operate in connection with an underlying Layer-1 blockchain.
  • Ethereum is the Layer-1 foundation for many Layer-2 systems.
  • Rollups are an important category of Layer-2 technology.
  • Optimistic rollups and zero-knowledge rollups use different approaches to verifying transaction results.
  • Layer-2 networks can reduce transaction costs by processing activity more efficiently.
  • Different Layer-2 networks have different technical and security assumptions.
  • Bridges and network selection are important considerations for users moving assets between blockchain environments.
